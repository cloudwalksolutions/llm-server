## The differential — the actual question

"Does KV quantization hurt tool calling more than it hurts text?" is a ratio,
not an absolute. Below is each metric on `tools` divided by the same metric on
`prose`, per arm. **1.0 means no tool-calling-specific effect.**

| arm | mean KLD | 99% KLD | 99.9% KLD | same top-1 (pp difference) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `kv8` | 1.06x | 1.24x | 1.27x | +0.35 pp |
| `kv4` | 1.21x | 1.47x | 1.75x | +0.70 pp |

Read `same top-1` as the behavioural metric — it is the share of tokens where
the quantized cache still ranks the same token first. A **positive** pp difference
means the tool-calling corpus held up BETTER than prose.

## How to read this

- **same top-1** is the share of tokens where the quantized cache still ranks
  the same token first. This is the number that tracks behaviour.
- **99% / 99.9% KLD** are the tail. A mean KLD near zero with a fat tail means
  most tokens are fine and a few are badly wrong — which is exactly how a
  malformed tool call happens.
- **The differential is the finding.** Compare each arm's numbers on `tools`
  against the same arm on `prose`. Similar degradation means KV quantization
  is not specifically a tool-calling problem, whatever the folklore says.
- **`k4v8` is the diagnostic.** llama.cpp #23470 measured q4_0-on-K destroying
  Qwen2.5-7B (PPL ratio 196x, same top-1 98% -> 11.8%) while q4_0-on-V cost
  almost nothing. If `k4v8` collapses here and `k8v4` does not, K sensitivity
  is the whole story and `cache_type_k` is the only knob that matters.
